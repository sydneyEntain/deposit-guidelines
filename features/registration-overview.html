Registration

Overview

This page references the registration journey that is currently live for Ladbrokes and Coral in the UK. The reg journey takes a new customer from "Welcome" through account creation, identity capture, age & identity verification, optional deposit limits, fund-protection acknowledgement, and marketing preferences, then into their first deposit. The persistent progress bar and offer-signposting banner that run across the flow, are essential reminders of the new customer offer they will be eligible for upon registration and terms fulfilment.

The first-time-deposit (FTD) step is a mirror image of the standard deposit flow with the exception of the bonus signposting and progress bar at the top of the page. The 'add-method' and deposit flows are documented and maintained in the Deposit guideline page; the FTD frames inside the Onboarding Figma file linked below are out of date and should not be used for reference.

Principles

  • Keep sign-up fast and low-friction ("Fast, secure and in just a few clicks.") while capturing exactly what regulation requires — no more.
  • Verify age and identity as a first-class, non-skippable part of the journey, using details "as shown on your ID".
  • Make responsible-gambling and fund-protection choices visible early: deposit limits and fund protection are surfaced during onboarding, not buried.
  • Give the customer control over how they're contacted — marketing is opt-in per channel, and blank means no marketing.
  • Signpost the new-customer offer consistently across steps without letting variable campaign copy dictate the layout.

Guidelines

Feature intent

A prospective customer wants to create an account and start playing. The flow must collect account credentials and verifiable identity details, satisfy age & identity verification and responsible-gambling obligations, let the customer set optional deposit limits and marketing preferences, acknowledge fund protection, and hand off to the first deposit and new-customer offer — all without losing the customer to unnecessary friction.

User mental model

  • Customers think "sign up, prove who I am, put some money in, start playing" — they expect a short, ordered sequence with visible progress.
  • They expect to enter their real details "as shown on ID" and understand this is for verification, not marketing.
  • They treat deposit limits and marketing as optional set-up they can change later, and expect that to be true.
  • They expect the advertised new-customer offer to still be there at the end, and to know what unlocks it.
  • They distinguish "creating the account" from "getting verified" and "making my first deposit" as separate milestones.

Onboarding flow & steps adaptation

The journey is a stepped flow tracked by the progress bar. Ordered steps:

  1. Splash page — brand landing with the new-customer offer surface, options for existing customers to Log In or for new customers to continue with account creation.
  2. Set up your account — collection of primary ACCOUNT information fields (email, phone number, username and password).
  3. Let's get to know you — collection of primary customer IDENTIFICATION fields (title, first name, last name, date of birth, address — including address lookup or manual entry).
  4. Age & Identity Verification + Account Creation — loading pages appear while the customer's data is being analyzed, age confirmed and account created. If this step is processed quickly in the backend, then the customer never sees these pages.
  5. Welcome page — formal welcome to the customer and confirmation their account has been created, clear reminder of their new customer offer and CTA that prompts them to continue.
  6. Deposit limits — interacting with this page is optional for customers and design here continues to evolve with the new regulations around Gross and Net limits.
  7. Fund Protection — customers must actively acknowledge the fund protection before being allowed to do anything on our platform.
  8. Marketing preferences — data has shown that customer value is higher for customers who opt-in to marketing so a decision was made to surface it here — this is not a requirement and the decision can be re-evaluated as needed.
  9. FTD (first time deposit) — this directs the customer into the deposit flow; while technically optional we want to strongly encourage the customer to deposit at registration. For details on the functionality and appearance of this page please reference the Deposit page.
  10. Landing / start playing — customer enters the landing/home page after successful deposit completion or after exiting the deposit section.

Persistent across steps: the progress bar component, the offer-signposting banner, and a footer with Help & Contact · Safer Gambling · 18+.

Offer surfaces (structure) adaptation

New-customer offer copy is variable campaign content and MUST NOT be transcribed as fixed. Any offer text seen in the designs (free-bet amounts, "when you bet £X", full 18+ terms) is sample data. Document the container — slots, limits, and required elements — not the campaign values.

Hard constraints

NEVER enable an account to gamble before age & identity verification is complete. Layer 1
NEVER allow an under-18 customer to create an active gambling account. Layer 1
NEVER opt a customer into marketing they did not explicitly select. Layer 1
NEVER proceed past fund protection without acknowledgement. Layer 1
NEVER bake offer copy or the "18+ T&Cs apply" disclosure into a background image as pixels. Layer 3
NEVER truncate the offer headline — require shorter copy instead. Layer 3
NEVER transcribe variable campaign offer values into this document as if fixed. Layer 3

Examples

Age & identity verification
Correct
The account is created but cannot gamble until age & identity are verified from the details entered "as shown on your ID".
Counter-example
Enabling deposits and betting before verification completes — violates R-001 and the Layer 1 constraint.
Marketing opt-in
Correct
The customer leaves the marketing options blank and receives no marketing communications.
Counter-example
Pre-ticking channels or enrolling the customer by default — violates R-005 / B-004.
Deposit limits optional
Correct
The customer selects "Set limits later" and continues; limits stay changeable in Account Settings.
Counter-example
Forcing a limit to be set before continuing, or hiding where to change it later — violates R-003 / B-003.
Offer surface (structure)
Correct
The hero headline fits its 2-line box and the "18+ T&Cs apply" disclosure is present as live text.
Counter-example
Baking the offer headline and terms into the background image, or truncating the headline — violates A-002 / B-006 and the Layer 3 constraints.
