Login

The live UK sign-in journey for Ladbrokes. An existing customer signs in with email/username and password, can stay signed in, recover a forgotten password, or route to account creation. Appears as a modal on desktop web and a full-screen sheet on mobile web; native apps add biometric login.

Overview

Login lives in Player Experience › Login and Access and is the shipped, live authentication journey for Ladbrokes (UK). It lets an existing customer sign in with an email/username and password, stay signed in, recover a forgotten password, and reach account creation if they don't yet have an account. On web it appears as a modal on desktop and a full-screen sheet on mobile — the same component at different screen sizes. Native apps additionally offer biometric login (see the native section).

Principles

Derived from the observed experience's recurring intent; confirm with the feature lead before approval.

  • Keep sign-in fast and single-screen: two fields, one primary action, everything else secondary.
  • Never dead-end a customer: a failed login always offers a next step (check details, reset password, or create an account).
  • Offer persistence on the customer's terms ("Keep me logged in" is off by default and explicit).
  • Make recovery reachable from the point of failure (forgotten-password and live-chat help are one tap away).
  • Keep authentication secure and honest — never reveal more than necessary about why a sign-in failed.

Guidelines

Feature intent

An existing customer wants to get into their account quickly and securely. The flow must accept an email or username plus password, validate input before enabling submission, communicate failure clearly without over-disclosing, offer password recovery, keep the customer signed in only when they choose, and route new users to registration — while satisfying the security and regulatory obligations of account access.

User mental model

  • Customers think "enter my details and get in" — they expect two fields and a button, nothing more up front.
  • They expect to use either an email or a username interchangeably in the first field.
  • They distinguish "I typed something wrong" from "I don't have an account" and expect the message to tell them which.
  • They expect a way back in if they've forgotten their password, and expect "keep me logged in" to be their choice.
  • Returning app users expect to use biometrics rather than retype credentials.

Login surfaces & variants adaptation

The same login logic is presented across three surfaces:

  • Desktop web (modal): opened from the header "Login"; overlays the page with a dimmed background and a close (X).
  • Mobile web (full-screen sheet): the same fields and actions rendered full-screen with a close (X). Field set and copy are identical to desktop.
  • Native app (biometric): in addition to email/username + password, native apps offer biometric sign-in (Face ID / fingerprint). [INFERRED — confirm affordance, enrolment, and fallback; not observable from web]

Forgotten password adaptation

Recovery is a separate screen reached from "I forgot my Password".

  • Heading: "Forgotten Password".
  • Fields: Email ("Please enter your email") and Date of birth as DD / MM / YYYY ("Please enter your date of birth").
  • Primary CTA: "SUBMIT" (disabled until valid).
  • Help: "Need help? Start Live Chat" and "Need more information on this? Have a look at our Password reset FAQ".

Hard constraints

NEVER reveal whether it was specifically the password that was wrong in a way that aids account enumeration beyond the observed messaging. Layer 1
NEVER grant access before any required additional authentication factor is completed. Layer 1
NEVER persist the session unless the customer has explicitly enabled "Keep me logged in". Layer 1
NEVER enable "LOG IN" while a required field is empty. Layer 3
NEVER dead-end a failed login — always offer recovery or account creation. Layer 3

Examples

No matching account
Correct
The customer sees "Account doesn't exist" and a "Create an account" route.
Counter-example
A generic "login failed" with no explanation or next step — violates B-003 and the no-dead-end principle.
Empty fields
Correct
Submitting with an empty field shows "Please enter your email or username" / "Please enter your password." and keeps the CTA disabled.
Counter-example
Allowing submit with empty fields or failing silently — violates B-001 / B-002.
Session persistence
Correct
"Keep me logged in" is off by default; the session persists only when the customer turns it on.
Counter-example
Persisting the session by default without consent — violates B-004 and the Layer 1 constraint.
Forgotten password
Correct
"I forgot my Password" opens recovery (email + date of birth) with live-chat help.
Counter-example
Hiding recovery or requiring a call to support — violates B-005 and the recovery principle.
