Withdrawals

Overview

Withdrawals is the money-out flow that allows a customer to remove funds from their account. It lives in Player Experience › Payments and is the sibling of Deposit as it reuses the same amount-entry module, custom keypad, and payment-method rows, adapted for money-out. This documentation covers amount entry (shared with Deposit), withdrawal-method selection, Money-In-Money-Out (MIMO) routing that splits withdrawals back to original deposit sources, adding/managing bank accounts, the no-eligible-method edge case, the approved / requested / partially-approved / declined outcome states and the header which shows the account Balance (e.g. "Balance: £350.00"), and on success the New balance (e.g. "New balance: £305.00").

Figma source: Ladbrokes Refresh — Payments Design / Delivery File — Withdrawals

Principles

  • Reuse the deposit amount-entry model so money-out feels consistent with money-in; only the direction and constraints change.
  • Return funds to where they came from by default (MIMO), splitting a withdrawal across original deposit sources so AML / source-of-funds rules are honoured without the customer having to think about it.
  • Never leave the customer without a path: when no method is eligible, explain plainly why and what unlocks withdrawals.
  • Be honest about timing and partial outcomes — approved, requested (pending), partially approved, declined, or failed — and always state where the customer's money is.

Guidelines

Feature intent

The customer wants to take money out of their account and understand when they'll receive it. The flow must make the withdrawable amount and range obvious, route funds back to eligible sources (MIMO), let the customer add or change a bank account where needed, and clearly communicate the outcome — including pending, partial, and declined cases — without ever misrepresenting whether their money has left the account.

User mental model

  • Customers think "how much do I want to take out, and where does it go" — amount and destination are the two things they expect to control.
  • They expect to be able to withdraw to whatever payment method they choose, but due to MIMO (money-in money-out) regulations, withdrawals must first be allocated to methods that were used to deposit (this needs to be clearly signposted).
  • They distinguish "approved / done" from "requested / still processing" and expect a realistic timeframe.
  • They expect that a declined withdrawal leaves their funds untouched.
  • Returning customers expect their default withdrawal method to be pre-selected; if they have no eligible method they expect to be told why.

Amount limits (computed) adaptation

The minimum and maximum shown in the amount helper ("£X min | £Y max") are not fixed — they are computed for the current session. Any concrete figures in the designs are sample data.

  • Minimum = the minimum for the selected payment method (each method has its own minimum).
  • Maximum = the lowest of three values: (1) the maximum for the selected payment method, (2) any customer-set limit that caps withdrawals, and (3) the customer's withdrawable balance.

Both bounds depend on the selected method, so the helper updates when the method changes. The max can be driven by a Responsible Gambling / account limit or by available balance — whichever is smallest wins.

Withdrawal methods & MIMO adaptation

Withdrawals use Money-In-Money-Out (MIMO): funds are returned to the sources they were deposited from, and a single withdrawal may be split across multiple destinations.

  • Split behaviour: the change-method / confirmation sheet shows "Withdrawal will be split to:" with a per-destination breakdown (e.g. Apple Pay • 2345 → £5.00, another method → £40.00).
No eligible method"You currently have no eligible withdrawal methods. To withdraw, you must first deposit using a payment method that allows deposits and withdrawals."

Bank account management adaptation

Bank accounts are added and managed within the withdrawal flow.

  • Add bank account ("Enter Bank details" screen): fields for the bank details (account name, sort code, account number and a paired field), a primary confirm CTA, and FAQs in the footer.
  • My withdrawal methods: lists stored bank/withdrawal methods with an "Add a withdrawal method" affordance.
  • Max bank accounts: a hard cap of 5 stored bank accounts — beyond that the customer must contact Customer Support.

Hard constraints

NEVER return withdrawn funds to a source that isn't eligible under MIMO. Layer 1
NEVER state or imply that funds have left the account when a withdrawal was declined — funds remain in the account. Layer 1
NEVER release a payout that requires identity/payment verification before that verification is complete. Layer 1
NEVER enable the Withdraw CTA when the amount is outside the allowed range. Layer 3
NEVER allow a 6th stored bank account to be added in-app — route to Customer Support. Layer 3

Examples

Declined withdrawal
Correct
On decline, the screen shows "£45.00 Withdrawal declined" and states "Your funds remain in your account", offering retry or another method.
Counter-example
A generic "transaction failed" with no confirmation that funds are still in the account — violates B-004 and the Layer 1 constraint.
MIMO split
Correct
Before confirming, the customer sees "Withdrawal will be split to:" with £40.00 to one source and £5.00 to another.
Counter-example
Silently splitting the payout across sources with no breakdown shown — violates B-003.
No eligible method
Correct
The customer is told they have no eligible withdrawal methods and that they must first deposit with a withdrawal-capable method.
Counter-example
An empty method list or a disabled Withdraw button with no explanation — violates B-002.
Requested (pending) state
Correct
"Withdrawal requested" with a payment-provider timeframe and a "Track withdrawal" action.
Counter-example
Showing "Withdrawal approved / complete" while the payout is still processing — violates B-005.
Sixth bank account
Correct
With 5 accounts stored, the customer is told to contact Customer Support to add another.
Counter-example
Letting a 6th bank account be added in-app — violates B-006.
